University Research Lab Quantum Optics

Custom Waveplates for Entangled Photon Source

Customer Application

Quantum information research group at a leading Chinese university, developing a polarization-entangled photon pair source based on spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) for quantum key distribution experiments.

Requirement

Matched zero-order quarter-wave plates at 810nm with retardance accuracy better than λ/200 and high extinction ratio for precision polarization state preparation. Standard catalog waveplates had insufficient retardance tolerance.

Engineering Challenge

Achieving retardance accuracy of λ/200 at 810nm required precise crystal thickness control and fast-axis alignment better than 0.1°. The MgF₂ zero-order design demanded clean handling and AR coating optimized for single-wavelength performance.

Material Selection

UV-grade crystalline quartz selected for birefringence stability at 810nm. MgF₂ used as alternative material for comparison. Crystal orientation verified by X-ray diffraction before processing.

Coating Selection

Single-wavelength AR coating at 810nm, R < 0.2% per surface, optimized for the specific crystal birefringence axis orientation.

Specification Optimization

Retardance tolerance tightened from standard λ/100 to λ/200 through iterative thickness measurement and trimming. Fast-axis alignment specified to ±0.1° with fiducial marks for reproducible mounting.

Manufacturing Route

Precision-cut crystalline quartz plates polished to optical quality, thickness trimmed to achieve λ/200 retardance at 810nm. AR coating applied after final dimension verification.

Inspection

Retardance measured by precision polarimeter at 810nm. Fast-axis angle verified by rotation stage with angular encoder. Surface quality and AR coating performance checked per specification.

Final Specification

Matched pair of zero-order waveplates in crystalline quartz, retardance accuracy λ/200 at 810nm, fast-axis alignment ±0.1°, single-wavelength AR coating (R < 0.2% at 810nm). Delivered with individual measurement certificates.

Result

Retardance accuracy achieved within λ/200 specification for all delivered units. Entanglement visibility improved significantly in Bell-state measurement. Fast-axis alignment enabled reproducible polarization state preparation across experimental runs.

Documentation

Individual retardance measurement report, fast-axis orientation certificate, coating performance data and material documentation provided.

Anonymized Engineering Project
Medical Device Manufacturer Diagnostic Imaging System

Precision Imaging Lens Assembly for Diagnostic Instrument

Customer Application

Medical diagnostic equipment manufacturer requiring a custom imaging lens for sensor-based diagnostic instruments operating in the visible spectrum (400–700nm).

Requirement

Custom 3-element imaging lens achieving diffraction-limited MTF across 400–700nm with less than 0.1% distortion. Tight centration tolerances required to maintain sensor image quality. Consistent performance across production batches.

Engineering Challenge

The customer’s initial single-vendor approach could not consistently meet MTF specifications across production batches. Achieving diffraction-limited performance with tight centration tolerances required coordinated optical design, precision manufacturing and rigorous incoming inspection.

Material Selection

BK7 and SF11 optical glass selected for the achromatic doublet plus correction element design. Material pairing chosen to correct chromatic aberration across the 400–700nm visible band while maintaining cost-effectiveness for medical device production volumes.

Coating Selection

Broadband AR coating optimized for 400–700nm visible range, applied to maximize transmission across all operational wavelengths of the diagnostic imaging system.

Specification Optimization

Through DFM review, center thickness tolerances on one element were identified as relaxable without affecting optical performance, reducing manufacturing cost. Cemented interface of the achromatic doublet optimized for minimum wavefront error. Centration specified to 30 arc-seconds.

Manufacturing Route

Precision polishing and centering of individual elements, cementing of achromatic doublet with optimized interface, and final assembly with interferometric verification of each element before assembly.

Inspection

Interferometric incoming inspection for each element before assembly. Final assembled-lens MTF verification across all fields and wavelengths. Distortion measurement confirmed compliance with specification.

Final Specification

3-element lens assembly (achromatic doublet + correction element) in BK7/SF11, centration to 30 arc-seconds, broadband visible AR coating. Delivered per project specification with full inspection documentation.

Result

MTF exceeded specification across all fields and wavelengths. Distortion measured well under 0.1% target. Batch-to-batch consistency maintained across multiple production runs. Significant cost reduction achieved through DFM tolerance optimization.

Documentation

Inspection report including interferometric data, MTF measurement results and material certification provided per project requirements.

Anonymized Engineering Project
National Research Institute Analytical Instrumentation

Custom Filter Set for Multi-Wavelength Raman System

Customer Application

Analytical instrumentation group at a national research institute developing a multi-wavelength Raman spectroscopy system for in-situ material characterization and process monitoring.

Requirement

Custom bandpass filter set matched to three excitation wavelengths (532nm, 633nm, 785nm) with high OD blocking, narrow bandwidth, and high transmission at Stokes shift region. Off-the-shelf filters had insufficient blocking depth and bandwidth mismatch.

Engineering Challenge

Achieving OD > 6 blocking with < 1% bandwidth (FWHM) while maintaining > 90% transmission at the target Stokes shift wavelengths required multi-cavity thin-film design with precise layer control. Thermal stability of coating performance under continuous laser illumination was also critical.

Material Selection

BK7 substrates selected for visible/NIR compatibility and cost-effectiveness. Fused silica substrates used for 532nm filters where laser-induced damage resistance was more critical.

Coating Selection

Multi-cavity bandpass designs with hard-ion-beam-assisted deposition for environmental stability. Each filter optimized for specific excitation wavelength with transmission window matched to expected Stokes shift range.

Specification Optimization

Bandwidth and edge steepness optimized through DFM review to balance spectral performance with manufacturing yield. Blocking depth specified at OD 6 across full blocking range. Out-of-band transmission verified across 200–1100nm.

Manufacturing Route

Precision substrate preparation followed by ion-beam-assisted deposition of multi-cavity coating designs. In-process monitoring of layer thickness during deposition. Post-coating annealing for stress relief.

Inspection

Spectrophotometric transmission and blocking measured across full wavelength range. Bandwidth and edge wavelength verified per specification. Laser durability assessed by extended exposure test.

Final Specification

Three matched bandpass filters at 532nm, 633nm and 785nm excitation, bandwidth < 1% FWHM, OD > 6 blocking, > 90% transmission at Stokes shift. BK7 and fused silica substrates. Delivered with full spectral characterization data.

Result

Blocking depth exceeded OD 6 across full spectral range. Stokes-shift transmission exceeded 90% target. Raman signal-to-background ratio improved significantly across all three excitation wavelengths. System enabled real-time process monitoring capability.

Documentation

Full spectral transmission curves, blocking characterization data, laser durability test report and material certificates provided.

Anonymized Engineering Project
Research Laboratory Ultrafast Pulse Compression

Custom CaF₂ Prism Pair for Femtosecond Laser System

Customer Application

University research lab specializing in ultrafast laser science, operating a Ti:Sapphire laser system at 800nm for pulse compression experiments.

Requirement

Matched CaF₂ prism pair for pulse compression with apex angle matching better than 1 arc-minute and exceptionally low wavefront distortion. Standard catalog prisms had too much angle variation between individual pieces.

Engineering Challenge

Achieving apex angle matching better than 1 arc-minute between two separate prisms required manufacturing both in the same batch using identical tooling and process parameters. Standard catalog prisms could not meet this level of matching.

Material Selection

CaF₂ (calcium fluoride) selected for its broad transmission range covering the Ti:Sapphire 800nm operating wavelength, low dispersion characteristics suitable for pulse compression, and compatibility with ultrafast laser applications.

Coating Selection

Broadband AR coating at 700–900nm optimized for P-polarization, matching the Ti:Sapphire system operating wavelength and polarization state.

Specification Optimization

Apex angle matching specification set to better than 1 arc-minute, with both prisms manufactured from the same CaF₂ boule using identical tooling to minimize differential error.

Manufacturing Route

Both prisms manufactured in the same batch using identical tooling and process parameters to ensure matching. Precision polishing followed by broadband AR coating deposition optimized for the 700–900nm band.

Inspection

Apex angle measurement via autocollimator for both prisms. Transmitted wavefront verified at 633nm. Matched pair delivered as a set with comparison data between the two prisms.

Final Specification

Matched CaF₂ prism pair with apex angle matching better than 1 arc-minute, broadband AR coating at 700–900nm (P-polarization optimized), verified transmitted wavefront at 633nm. Delivered per project specification with full inspection documentation.

Result

Apex angle matching achieved well within the 1 arc-minute target. Transmitted wavefront quality met specifications per prism. Pulse compression successfully achieved. A second identical pair was subsequently ordered for an expanded beam setup.

Documentation

Inspection report including autocollimator apex angle comparison data, transmitted wavefront measurements and material documentation provided per project requirements.

Anonymized Engineering Project
University Laser Laboratory Ultrafast Optics

Custom CaF₂ Optics for High-Repetition-Rate Femtosecond System

Customer Application

Ultrafast laser laboratory at a research university operating a 1MHz repetition-rate Yb:femto fiber laser system at 1030nm for high-field physics experiments and attosecond pulse generation.

Requirement

Custom CaF₂ compressor optics including chirped mirrors and Brewster-angle windows with group delay dispersion (GDD) precision better than ±5 fs² across the operating bandwidth. Standard optics had GDD deviation exceeding specifications.

Engineering Challenge

Achieving GDD accuracy of ±5 fs² at 1030nm required sub-nanometer control of coating layer thickness and precise CaF₂ substrate characterization. The broad operating bandwidth (1010–1050nm) demanded coating design that maintained GDD flatness across 40nm bandwidth while handling 100W average power.

Material Selection

UV-grade CaF₂ selected for low absorption at 1030nm and excellent homogeneity. Material batch verified for absorption coefficient and homogeneity before processing.

Coating Selection

Custom chirped mirror coating design (35+ layer pairs) optimized for negative GDD across 1010–1050nm band. Ion-beam sputtering used for precise thickness control. Brewster windows uncoated with surface quality optimized for minimum scatter loss.

Specification Optimization

GDD flatness tolerance optimized through iterative design-manufacture-measure cycles. Substrate wedge angle specified for Brewster windows to minimize beam deviation. Surface quality specified at 20-10 to minimize intra-cavity loss.

Manufacturing Route

CaF₂ substrates precision-cut and polished to specification. Chirped mirror coating deposited by ion-beam sputtering with in-situ optical monitoring. Brewster windows precision-polished at specified wedge angle.

Inspection

GDD measured by white-light interferometer across full bandwidth. Reflectivity and phase verified per specification. Surface quality inspected under calibrated illumination. Absorption measured by photothermal deflection.

Final Specification

Custom CaF₂ chirped mirrors with GDD -50 ± 5 fs² across 1010–1050nm, reflectivity > 99.5%. CaF₂ Brewster windows at specified wedge angle, surface quality 20-10. All components delivered with individual GDD characterization data.

Result

GDD flatness achieved within ±5 fs² across the full 40nm bandwidth. Pulse compression successfully achieved sub-100fs output. Intra-cavity loss minimized by high-quality Brewster windows. System enabled stable high-repetition-rate femtosecond operation for high-field experiments.

Documentation

Individual GDD measurement curves, reflectivity spectra, surface quality reports and material certificates provided.

Anonymized Engineering Project
Laser Systems OEM 1064nm Laser Cavity

Custom 1064nm High-Reflectivity Mirror for Laser Cavity

Customer Application

Laser manufacturer building pulsed Nd:YAG laser systems requiring high-reflectivity cavity mirrors for resonator applications at 1064nm.

Requirement

HR mirrors with reflectivity above 99.8% at 1064nm, damage threshold above 10 J/cm², and wavefront distortion below λ/20. Previous supplier could only achieve 99.5% reflectivity with inconsistent damage thresholds.

Engineering Challenge

Achieving simultaneously high reflectivity, high damage threshold and low wavefront distortion required coordinated substrate preparation, multi-layer dielectric coating design and Ion-Assisted Deposition process control. The previous supplier’s inconsistent results indicated process limitations in coating density and adhesion.

Material Selection

Premium fused silica substrates selected for low thermal expansion, high laser damage resistance and excellent surface finish capability at 1064nm.

Coating Selection

35-layer HfO₂/SiO₂ dielectric coating stack optimized for 1064nm at 0° AOI. HfO₂ selected as high-index material for its superior laser damage resistance compared to alternative metal oxides. Ion-Assisted Deposition (IAD) used for dense, stable films.

Specification Optimization

Substrate flatness specified at λ/20 with 20-10 surface quality to ensure wavefront distortion remained within budget after coating. Coating layer design optimized for maximum reflectivity at 1064nm while maintaining damage threshold above requirement.

Manufacturing Route

Premium fused silica substrates polished to λ/20 flatness with 20-10 surface quality. 35-layer HfO₂/SiO₂ dielectric coating deposited using Ion-Assisted Deposition (IAD) for dense, stable films with high laser damage resistance.

Inspection

100% LIDT testing at 10 J/cm². Reflectivity measured at 1064nm. Wavefront distortion verified via interferometry. Each mirror underwent full inspection prior to shipment.

Final Specification

Premium fused silica HR mirror with 35-layer HfO₂/SiO₂ dielectric coating, reflectivity above 99.8% at 1064nm, LIDT above 10 J/cm², λ/20 flatness, 20-10 surface quality. Delivered per project specification with full inspection documentation.

Result

Reflectivity exceeded 99.8% specification. LIDT exceeded damage threshold requirement. Wavefront distortion measured better than λ/20 specification. Batch consistency maintained across multiple production runs.

Documentation

Inspection report including reflectivity measurement, LIDT test results, interferometric wavefront data and material certification provided per project requirements.

Anonymized Engineering Project
Medical University Biomedical Imaging

Custom Broadband Optics for Spectral-Domain OCT System

Customer Application

Biomedical optics research group at a medical university developing a spectral-domain optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT) system for ophthalmic imaging at 840nm center wavelength with 100nm bandwidth.

Requirement

Custom broadband beam splitter and reference mirror optimized for 790–890nm OCT band with high splitting ratio uniformity and flat phase response across full bandwidth. Standard components introduced wavelength-dependent artifacts in OCT images.

Engineering Challenge

Maintaining < 1% splitting ratio variation across 100nm bandwidth (790–890nm) while achieving flat group delay for coherent imaging required broadband coating design with precise thickness control. The reference mirror needed high reflectivity with minimal dispersion across the full OCT bandwidth.

Material Selection

BK7 substrates selected for good transmission in the 840nm OCT band. Fused silica reference mirror substrate used for low thermal expansion and excellent surface finish capability.

Coating Selection

Broadband beam splitter coating designed for 50/50 splitting ratio across 790–890nm with < 1% variation. Reference mirror coating optimized for R > 99% across same band with flat group delay response. Hard coating process for environmental stability.

Specification Optimization

Coating design iterated to achieve splitting ratio flatness. Reference mirror GDD specified to minimize dispersion-induced image degradation. Surface quality optimized for minimum scatter in interferometric imaging.

Manufacturing Route

Precision substrate preparation and polishing. Broadband coating deposited by ion-assisted deposition with in-situ monitoring. Environmental stability verified by temperature cycling test.

Inspection

Splitting ratio measured across full 790–890nm band. Reflectivity and GDD verified for reference mirror. Surface quality and coating adhesion checked per specification. System-level interferometric performance verified in customer’s OCT setup.

Final Specification

BK7 broadband beam splitter with 50/50 splitting ratio (±1% across 790–890nm). Fused silica reference mirror with R > 99% and flat GDD across 790–890nm. Surface quality 20-10 on all optical surfaces. Delivered with full spectral characterization data.

Result

Splitting ratio uniformity achieved within ±1% across full OCT bandwidth. Reference mirror dispersion minimized, enabling improved axial resolution. OCT image quality significantly improved with reduced wavelength-dependent artifacts. System achieved sub-5μm axial resolution in ophthalmic imaging tests.

Documentation

Splitting ratio spectral curve, reference mirror reflectivity and GDD data, surface quality reports and material certificates provided.

Anonymized Engineering Project
Medical Device Manufacturer Endoscopic Imaging System

Biocompatible Sapphire Window for Endoscopic Imaging

Customer Application

Medical device manufacturer developing a reusable endoscope requiring a distal window that maintains optical clarity through repeated clinical use and sterilization cycles.

Requirement

Distal window capable of withstanding repeated autoclave sterilization (134°C, 2 bar), resistant to scratching from surgical tools, and maintaining optical clarity. Custom 3.5mm diameter with 0.5mm thickness. Previous BK7 and standard glass windows were failing due to surface damage after 20–30 sterilization cycles.

Engineering Challenge

Standard optical glasses (BK7) could not survive repeated autoclave sterilization cycles and were susceptible to surgical instrument scratching. The combination of extreme hardness requirement, biocompatibility, autoclave resistance and small custom dimensions presented a multi-constraint material and manufacturing challenge.

Material Selection

Optical-grade sapphire (Al₂O₃) selected as the ideal material — 9H hardness immune to surgical instrument scratching, material compatibility evaluated per ISO 10993 for biocompatibility, and inherently resistant to autoclave sterilization conditions (134°C, 2 bar).

Coating Selection

Broadband AR coating at 400–700nm applied to maintain optical clarity for visible imaging through the endoscope. Coating selected for adhesion and durability on sapphire substrate through repeated autoclave cycles.

Specification Optimization

Flatness specified at λ/4 for endoscopic imaging quality. Custom dimensions of 3.5mm diameter with 0.5mm thickness achieved through precision processing of sapphire. Specifications reviewed and optimized for the sapphire material characteristics.

Manufacturing Route

Sapphire crystal precision cut and polished to λ/4 flatness. Custom 3.5mm diameter with 0.5mm thickness achieved through specialized sapphire processing techniques. Broadband visible AR coating applied to both surfaces.

Inspection

Flatness, surface quality, transmission and coating adhesion verified per project specification. Scratch resistance and autoclave durability validated through accelerated lifecycle testing.

Final Specification

Optical-grade sapphire (Al₂O₃) window, 3.5mm diameter × 0.5mm thickness, λ/4 flatness, broadband AR coating (400–700nm), biocompatible per ISO 10993 evaluation. Delivered per project specification with full inspection documentation.

Result

Survived extensive autoclave cycling with zero surface degradation. No measurable transmission loss after sterilization testing. Scratch resistance confirmed — no damage from standard surgical tool contact. Material compatibility verified per ISO 10993 evaluation.

Documentation

Inspection report, material certification, biocompatibility evaluation documentation and sterilization cycle test results provided per project requirements.
