Surface Quality Specifications: Understanding Scratch-Dig and Beyond

Complete guide to optical surface quality — from MIL-PRF-13830B scratch-dig to ISO 10110-7. Learn to specify the right quality without over-specifying cost.

QualitySurface FinishScratch-DigISO 10110
Published: August 23, 2026By PhotonEdge Engineering Team~8 min read

What Are Scratch-Dig Numbers?

MIL-PRF-13830B uses two numbers: scratch width (× 0.01mm) and dig diameter (× 0.01mm). '60-40' means max scratch width 0.6mm, max dig diameter 0.4mm.

First Number: Scratch

Scratch number × 0.01mm = max width. "60" = 0.6mm max width.

Second Number: Dig

Dig number × 0.01mm = max diameter. "40" = 0.4mm max diameter.

Common Grades & Cost Impact

GradeUseCostApplications
80-50EconomyBaseWindows, beam steering
60-40Standard+10-15%Imaging (f/8+), mirrors, prisms
40-20Precision+30-50%Imaging (f/4+), laser optics
20-10High precision+100-200%High-power laser, lithography
Rule of Thumb: Every step finer adds 30-100% polishing cost. 60-40 to 40-20 is the most common over-specification trap.

When Does It Matter?

High impact: High-power laser (scratches reduce LDT), precision imaging at f/2 or faster, interferometric surfaces.

Low impact: Non-imaging windows, mirrors at f/8+, prisms in fold mirrors, any defect smaller than the Airy disk.

Beyond Scratch-Dig

  • Surface roughness (Ra/RMS): Measured with profilometer. 0.5-2nm standard, <0.2nm superpolish. Directly correlates with scatter.
  • Total Integrated Scatter (TIS): Ratio of scattered to total reflected light. TIS < 0.1% for precision optics.
  • ISO 10110-7 'N' count: Specifies max defects per area. More objective than scratch-dig for production QC.

Practical Advice

Recommendation: For most commercial applications, 60-40 with visual inspection per MIL-PRF-13830B is sufficient. Only move to tighter grades when your application specifically requires it. For production consistency, add quantitative Ra or TIS alongside scratch-dig.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from optical engineers and procurement teams.

What does '60-40 scratch-dig' mean?+
Per MIL-PRF-13830B: max scratch width = 0.6mm (60 × 0.01), max dig diameter = 0.4mm (40 × 0.01). Lower = finer. 80-50 economy, 60-40 standard, 40-20 precision, 20-10 high precision.
How is surface quality inspected?+
Visual comparison against calibrated reference standards under dark-field illumination at 45°. Inherently subjective — specify quantitative Ra for critical applications.
When to use ISO 10110-7 vs MIL-PRF-13830B?+
ISO 10110-7 for European customers or ISO quality systems. MIL-PRF-13830B for US military/aerospace. Many modern specs reference both.
Does surface quality affect laser performance?+
Yes — scratches reduce laser damage threshold. A 20-10 surface can have 2-3x higher LDT than 60-40 at the same coating.
How much does tighter surface quality cost?+
80-50 to 60-40: +10-15%. 60-40 to 40-20: +30-50%. 40-20 to 20-10: +100-200%. Always specify the loosest grade your application allows.
Can I specify surface quality without scratch-dig?+
Yes. ISO 10110-7 offers 'N' classification. You can also specify surface roughness (Ra) or total integrated scatter (TIS) instead.
