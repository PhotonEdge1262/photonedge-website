Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • CVD ZnSe (multispectral grade) — High purity, low absorption. Standard for CO2 laser optics. 0.6–20µm transmission.
  • Cleartran ZnSe — Premium grade with lowest absorption. For high-power CO2 laser systems >2kW.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • AR @ 10.6µm — R < 0.2% per surface. Standard for CO2 laser focusing and beam delivery.
  • AR @ 9.3µm — R < 0.2% per surface. For CO2 laser systems operating at 9.3µm.
  • Dual-band AR @ 10.6/9.3µm — R < 0.3% at both wavelengths. For multi-line CO2 systems.
  • Uncoated — ~17% Fresnel loss per surface due to high refractive index (n≈2.4). Only for alignment or low-power.

Engineering Considerations

Thermal properties: ZnSe has relatively high thermal expansion (7.1 × 10⁻⁶/K) and dn/dT of 60 × 10⁻⁶/K. At high CO₂ laser powers (>1kW), thermal lensing becomes significant. Design your beam delivery with this in mind.

Handling: ZnSe is relatively soft (Mohs 4) and susceptible to scratching. Handle with care, use clean compressed air for cleaning rather than wiping. Avoid contact with acidic or alkaline solutions.

Meniscus vs plano-convex: For high-power CO₂ systems, meniscus lenses distribute heat more evenly and reduce thermal stress compared to plano-convex designs. Consider meniscus for powers above 2kW.

ZnSe is the standard for CO2 laser optics. This guide helps confirm it matches your wavelength and application.

Summary
ZnSe is the right choice for CO2 laser (10.6um) systems and broadband IR applications from visible to LWIR. If your system operates exclusively in the MWIR band with weight constraints, consider silicon. For NIR laser systems, fused silica is more economical.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum laser power a ZnSe lens can handle?
With proper AR coating and adequate beam diameter, CVD ZnSe lenses can handle >5kW CW CO2 laser power. The key limitation is absorption-induced heating. Ensure beam diameter is large enough to keep power density below the coating damage threshold.
How do I clean ZnSe optics?
Use clean, dry compressed air or nitrogen to blow off particles first. For fingerprints or residue, use high-purity isopropyl alcohol and gentle blotting (not rubbing). Avoid acetone, which can damage AR coatings on ZnSe.
What is the difference between CVD ZnSe and Cleartran ZnSe?
Cleartran is a premium CVD ZnSe grade with lower absorption coefficient (<0.003/cm vs <0.005/cm). For high-power systems >2kW, Cleartran reduces thermal lensing. For most cutting and marking applications, standard CVD ZnSe is sufficient.
