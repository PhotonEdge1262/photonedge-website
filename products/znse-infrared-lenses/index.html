Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • CVD ZnSe (Standard) — Multi-spectral grade. Broadest IR transmission. Standard for CO2 laser and thermal imaging.
  • CVD ZnSe (CO2 laser grade) — Optimized for 10.6um with lowest absorption. Highest LIDT for CO2 laser cutting and welding.
  • Multi-spectral ZnSe — For systems spanning visible alignment (600nm+) through LWIR. Allows HeNe or visible alignment beam.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • AR @ 10.6um (CO2) — R < 0.5% per surface. Standard for CO2 laser focusing and beam delivery.
  • DLC (Diamond-Like Carbon) — Hard protective overcoat. Essential for workshop environments. Prevents coating degradation from moisture and handling.
  • BB AR (0.6-18um) — R < 2% across full transmission range. For multi-wavelength IR systems with visible alignment.
  • AR @ 3-5um (MWIR) — R < 0.5% per surface. For MWIR thermal imaging with visible alignment capability.

Engineering Considerations

ZnSe vs Ge for CO2 lasers: ZnSe is the standard material for CO2 laser (10.6um) optics because it transmits from 600nm (visible red alignment beam possible) to 18um. Germanium is opaque below 2um and cannot pass visible alignment beams. For CO2 laser systems, ZnSe is preferred.

DLC coating is recommended: ZnSe is moderately soft (Mohs ~3.5) and susceptible to environmental degradation. DLC (diamond-like carbon) overcoat dramatically improves scratch resistance and environmental durability, especially for industrial laser workshop environments.

Temperature limitation: ZnSe begins to absorb significantly above ~120 degrees C. For high-average-power CO2 laser systems (>2kW), monitor optic temperature and consider active cooling or alternative materials for the output optics.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is ZnSe preferred over germanium for CO2 laser optics?
ZnSe transmits from 600nm to 18um, allowing visible alignment beams (red HeNe at 633nm) to pass through the optic for system alignment. Germanium is opaque below 2um, making alignment impossible without separate visible path. ZnSe also has lower absorption at 10.6um, reducing thermal lensing.
Is ZnSe hygroscopic?
ZnSe is not significantly hygroscopic, but it can degrade in humid environments over time, especially uncoated. DLC overcoat provides excellent environmental protection. Store optics in dry conditions when not in use.
