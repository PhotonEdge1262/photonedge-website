Germanium Infrared Lenses

Single-crystal germanium lenses for mid-wave and long-wave infrared optical systems operating from 2um to 14um. High refractive index (n=4.0) enables compact, fast f-number designs ideal for thermal imaging, FLIR systems, and IR spectroscopy.

Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • Germanium (Ge) — Highest refractive index among common IR materials. Compact designs possible. Standard for 8-14um thermal imaging.
  • ZnSe (Alternative) — Better transmission in MWIR (3-5um). Lower refractive index requires thicker elements. Hygroscopic at elevated temperatures.
  • Silicon (Si) — Lighter weight than Ge. Better for MWIR systems. Transmits 1.2-7um. Lower cost alternative when density is a concern.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • AR @ 8-12um (LWIR) — R < 0.5% per surface. Standard for thermal imaging systems at 8-14um.
  • AR @ 3-5um (MWIR) — R < 0.5% per surface. For MWIR gas sensing and thermal imaging.
  • DLC (Diamond-Like Carbon) — Hard, protective overcoat for harsh environments. Extends coating lifetime.
  • BB AR MWIR+LWIR — R < 1% across 3-14um. For systems operating in both atmospheric windows.

Engineering Considerations

Temperature limitation: Germanium becomes absorptive above approximately 100 degrees C due to intrinsic carrier generation. For high-temperature environments (>80 degrees C ambient), consider Si or ZnSe alternatives, or implement active cooling.

AR coating is mandatory: With n=4.0, uncoated Ge reflects ~36% per surface. AR coating is essential for any practical system - it improves throughput from 41% to >99% per element.

Weight consideration: Ge density (5.32 g/cm3) is over 2x that of BK7. For large-aperture systems (>50mm diameter), weight becomes significant. Consider Silicon (2.33 g/cm3) for weight-sensitive applications in the MWIR band.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does germanium have such a high refractive index?
Germanium is a semiconductor with a very high dielectric constant in the IR region, resulting in n=4.0 at 10.6um. This high index is advantageous - it allows stronger optical power with less curvature, enabling more compact lens designs.
Can germanium lenses be used at 1064nm?
No. Germanium is opaque below approximately 2um. It cannot be used for Nd:YAG (1064nm) or any visible/NIR laser applications. Use fused silica or BK7 for those wavelengths.
