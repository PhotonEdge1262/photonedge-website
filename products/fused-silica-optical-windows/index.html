Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • UV Grade Fused Silica — High OH content, excellent UV transmission down to 185nm. Ideal for excimer lasers and UV lithography.
  • N-BK7 — Cost-effective for visible-NIR applications. Good optical homogeneity for general-purpose windows.
  • CaF₂ (Calcium Fluoride) — Broad UV-IR transmission (130nm–10µm). Low refractive index, excellent for fluorescence and Raman spectroscopy.
  • Sapphire (Al₂O₃) — Extreme hardness (Mohs 9). Survives high-pressure and high-temperature environments. 150–5500nm transmission.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • Broadband AR (UV-VIS) — R < 0.25% per surface, 193–700nm. Maximizes throughput for multi-wavelength systems.
  • V-band AR (355nm) — R < 0.1% @ 355nm. Optimized for Nd:YAG third harmonic laser systems.
  • BBAR (Broadband) — R < 0.5% across 350–2000nm. Single coating for wide spectral coverage.
  • Uncoated — Suitable for low-power applications or where coating absorption is a concern. ~4% Fresnel loss per surface.
  • Custom coating — Design your coating for specific wavelength, angle of incidence and polarization requirements.

Engineering Considerations

Thermal management: Fused silica has near-zero thermal expansion (CTE ≈ 0.55 × 10⁻⁶/K), making it ideal for environments with temperature variation. However, at high average powers, even small absorption in coatings can cause thermal lensing.

LIDT considerations: Always specify your laser wavelength, pulse duration, repetition rate and beam diameter. Laser-induced damage threshold depends on all of these. A coating optimized for 355nm CW may fail at 355nm pulsed.

Surface quality vs. cost: 10-5 scratch-dig is needed for high-precision imaging and laser cavities. For simple protective windows, 60-40 is sufficient and significantly less expensive.

Wedging: For laser cavity windows, specify wedged windows (typically 0.5°–3°) to eliminate etalon effects that cause power instability.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between UV fused silica and N-BK7 for optical windows?
UV fused silica transmits from 185nm with high laser damage threshold and near-zero thermal expansion. N-BK7 is more economical for visible-NIR applications but absorbs below 350nm and has higher thermal expansion. For UV laser systems or high-power applications, fused silica is the standard choice.
What surface quality should I specify for my window?
For laser cavity windows and precision imaging: 10-5. For general beam paths and protective windows: 40-20 or 60-40 is sufficient. Tighter surface quality increases cost significantly, so specify only what your system actually requires.
Can you provide windows with custom dimensions?
Yes. We manufacture custom optical windows to your exact specifications including diameter, thickness, wedge angle, edge profile and coating. Typical lead time for custom windows is 2–4 weeks depending on complexity and quantity.
What is the laser damage threshold of your AR-coated fused silica windows?
Typical LIDT for our UV BBAR coated fused silica windows is >10 J/cm² at 1064nm (10ns pulse). For specific wavelengths and pulse parameters, we provide measured LIDT data per ISO 21254. Contact us with your laser parameters for exact specifications.
