Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • N-BK7 (K9) — Standard choice for visible-NIR prism applications. Excellent homogeneity, low cost, wide availability.
  • UV Fused Silica — Upgrade for UV transmission or high-power laser applications. Higher LIDT and broader transmission.
  • CaF2 — For UV excimer and broadband dispersion applications. Ultra-low GVD for ultrafast systems.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • BBAR VIS — R < 0.5% across 400-700nm. Maximizes throughput for visible multi-prism systems.
  • AR @ 532nm — R < 0.1% per surface. For green laser beam steering and deviation.
  • HR Reflective — Enhanced aluminum or protected silver for total internal reflection replacement at specific angles.
  • Uncoated — ~4% Fresnel loss per uncoated surface. Acceptable for non-critical beam paths.

Engineering Considerations

Prism type selection: Choose the prism geometry based on your function. Right angle prisms use total internal reflection (TIR) for 90-degree beam deviation. Dove prisms rotate images. Penta prisms deviate beams by exactly 90 degrees regardless of incident angle. Equilateral prisms provide angular dispersion for spectroscopy.

TIR wavelength limit: Total internal reflection works only when the beam hits the reflective surface above the critical angle. For BK7 at uncoated surfaces, TIR works well in the visible-NIR but check angles for your specific wavelength and geometry.

Beam deviation vs. dispersion: If you need beam steering without dispersion, use a right angle or penta prism. If you need spectral separation, use an equilateral or dispersing prism at the appropriate angle of incidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a right angle prism and a penta prism?
Both deviate the beam 90 degrees, but a right angle prism is sensitive to angular alignment errors (the deviation changes if the prism rotates), while a penta prism maintains exactly 90 degrees deviation regardless of rotation. Use penta prisms when angular accuracy is critical, such as in surveying instruments.
Can BK7 prisms be used for UV applications?
BK7 transmits down to about 330nm. For deeper UV (< 300nm), use UV fused silica or CaF2 prisms instead. BK7 is suitable for near-UV (330-400nm) with acceptable transmission.
