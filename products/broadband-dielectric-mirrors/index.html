Engineering Considerations

Bandwidth vs. reflectance tradeoff: Broader reflectance bands require more coating layers, which increases cost and can reduce peak reflectance. Specify the minimum bandwidth you need rather than the broadest possible range.

Angle sensitivity: Broadband dielectric coatings are more sensitive to angle of incidence than narrowband designs. The reflectance band shifts to shorter wavelengths at higher AoI. Specify your operating AoI — we optimize the coating for your specific angle.

GDD for ultrafast: For femtosecond laser systems, group delay dispersion (GDD) matters. Broadband chirped mirrors can provide controlled GDD for pulse compression. Specify GDD requirements for ultrafast applications.

Summary
Broadband dielectric mirrors are ideal when you need high reflectivity across a wide wavelength range — common in tunable lasers, multi-harmonic systems, and broadband imaging. For single-wavelength laser cavities requiring maximum reflectance, laser-line dielectric coatings achieve >99.99%. For the broadest spectrum at lowest cost, protected metal mirrors are the choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between broadband dielectric and metallic mirrors?
Metallic mirrors (enhanced Al, protected Ag) offer moderate reflectance (92-98%) across very broad ranges. Broadband dielectric mirrors offer higher reflectance (>99%) but over narrower defined bands. Choose metallic for maximum bandwidth, dielectric for maximum reflectance in a defined band.
Can broadband mirrors be used at wavelengths outside the design band?
Outside the design band, reflectance drops significantly — possibly below 80%. Never assume a broadband mirror works outside its specified range. Specify all wavelengths you need covered.
