Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • BK7 — Standard substrate for visible-NIR bandpass filters. Cost-effective and widely available.
  • UV Fused Silica — For UV bandpass filters (< 400nm) or applications requiring higher LIDT.
  • Borosilicate (BK7 equivalent) — Alternative substrate with similar optical properties. Good for fluorescence and machine vision.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • Narrow Bandpass (FWHM 1-10nm) — Hard-coated multi-cavity interference design. For laser line selection, Raman spectroscopy and fluorescence.
  • Standard Bandpass (FWHM 10-40nm) — Multi-cavity design for machine vision, sensor filtering and wavelength isolation.
  • Broadband Bandpass (FWHM 40-100nm) — Fewer cavity design for broad spectral selection. VIS, NIR or custom ranges.
  • Multi-band Bandpass — Dual or triple passband in a single filter. For multi-fluorophore imaging.

Engineering Considerations

Bandwidth selection: Narrower bandwidth (FWHM 1-3nm) provides better spectral isolation but lower peak transmission and higher cost. Choose the widest bandwidth that still provides adequate signal-to-noise for your application.

Angle tuning: Bandpass filters shift to shorter wavelengths when tilted from normal incidence (~0.1-0.3% per degree). This can be used for fine wavelength tuning, but for fixed-wavelength applications, maintain consistent AoI.

Blocking level: OD 4 (10,000:1 rejection) is sufficient for most applications. For fluorescence with strong excitation sources, OD 6 may be needed to suppress excitation leakage. Specify both the passband and the blocking range.

Summary
Bandpass filters are the right choice when you need to isolate a specific wavelength band with sharp cut-on and cut-off characteristics. If you need uniform intensity reduction across the spectrum, use neutral density filters. For beam-combining or separating applications where you need both transmission and reflection paths, dichroic filters are the solution.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between narrow bandpass and broadband filters?
Narrow bandpass filters (FWHM 1-10nm) transmit only a very narrow wavelength range, ideal for laser line selection and high-resolution spectroscopy. Broadband filters (FWHM 40-100nm) transmit a wider range, suitable for general spectral isolation and machine vision.
How does tilt angle affect bandpass filter performance?
Tilting a bandpass filter shifts the center wavelength to shorter wavelengths and can broaden the passband. For precise wavelength selection, mount the filter at normal incidence. For fine tuning, small angles (5-15 degrees) can shift the passband by a few nanometers.
