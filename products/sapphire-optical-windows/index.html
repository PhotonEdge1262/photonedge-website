Sapphire Optical Windows

Single-crystal sapphire (Al2O3) optical windows offering unmatched hardness (Mohs 9) and exceptional transmission from 180nm to 4.5um. Ideal for harsh environments, high-pressure viewports, and applications requiring extreme scratch resistance and chemical inertness.

Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • Sapphire (Al2O3) — Ultimate scratch resistance and chemical inertness. For harsh environments, high pressure, and abrasive conditions.
  • UV Fused Silica (Alternative) — Better UV transmission below 200nm. Lower hardness but more economical for non-abrasive applications.
  • ZnSe (IR Alternative) — Better transmission beyond 4.5um to 18um. Softer material - not suitable for abrasive environments.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • BBAR VIS-NIR — R < 0.5% across 400-1700nm. Maximizes throughput for visible and NIR systems.
  • AR @ 1064nm — For Nd:YAG laser system viewports in harsh environments.
  • AR UV-VIS — R < 0.25% across 200-700nm. For UV-visible spectroscopy in demanding environments.
  • Uncoated — ~7% Fresnel loss per surface (due to high n=1.77). Acceptable for protective cover glass.

Manufacturing Tolerances

We optimize specifications to match your system requirements — not over-specify. Tighter tolerances increase cost without always improving system performance. Typical manufacturing capabilities:

Engineering Considerations

Birefringence: Sapphire is birefringent (delta-n = 0.008). For polarized light systems, this can cause unwanted polarization rotation. Account for crystal axis orientation in your design. For unpolarized applications, birefringence is not a concern.

Machining difficulty: Sapphire's extreme hardness makes it more expensive to machine than BK7 or fused silica. Complex geometries (thin edges, small features) significantly increase cost. Keep designs as simple as possible.

Thermal shock: Despite high temperature capability, sapphire can crack under rapid temperature changes due to thermal stress. Specify gradual temperature transitions in your system design.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does sapphire compare to glass in scratch resistance?
Sapphire (Mohs 9) is dramatically harder than any optical glass. BK7 is approximately Mohs 5-6. In practical terms, sapphire will resist scratching from sand, dust, and most industrial abrasives that would quickly fog a glass window.
Can sapphire windows be used in vacuum systems?
Yes. Sapphire is compatible with ultra-high vacuum (UHV) environments. Its low outgassing and chemical inertness make it suitable for semiconductor processing and space applications.
What is the maximum pressure differential a sapphire window can handle?
Depends on diameter, thickness, and edge condition. A 25mm diameter x 3mm thick sapphire window can typically withstand >50 bar differential pressure. Contact us with your specific pressure requirements for engineering review.
