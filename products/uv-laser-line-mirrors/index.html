Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • UV Fused Silica (JGS1) — Highest purity, best UV transmission and laser damage threshold. Standard for excimer and UV Nd:YAG laser mirrors.
  • UV Fused Silica (JGS2) — Lower OH content, suitable for 248nm and above. More economical than JGS1 for non-193nm applications.
  • CaF2 (Specialty) — For 193nm excimer systems requiring lowest absorption. Higher cost but superior UV performance.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • HR @ 193nm (ArF) — R > 99.5%. IBS coating optimized for ArF excimer laser. Highest LIDT at 193nm.
  • HR @ 248nm (KrF) — R > 99.5%. For KrF excimer laser systems. Excellent long-term UV stability.
  • HR @ 266nm — R > 99.5%. For Nd:YAG 4th harmonic. Common in marking and microscopy.
  • HR @ 355nm — R > 99.5%. For Nd:YAG 3rd harmonic. Used in laser marking, LIDAR and scientific research.
  • HR @ 308nm (XeCl) — R > 99.5%. For XeCl excimer laser systems.

Engineering Considerations

IBS vs e-beam coating: For UV laser mirrors, ion-beam sputtered (IBS) coatings are strongly preferred. IBS produces denser, more uniform coatings with 2-5x higher laser damage threshold than conventional e-beam coatings. The higher initial cost pays for itself in longer mirror lifetime and reduced downtime.

Substrate material matters: At 193nm, even small amounts of impurities in fused silica cause absorption and reduced LIDT. Specify JGS1 (highest purity) for 193nm systems. JGS2 is acceptable for 248nm and longer wavelengths.

Angle of incidence: Dielectric HR coatings are designed for specific angles of incidence. Performance degrades at angles significantly different from the design AoI. Specify your AoI when ordering — 0 degrees (normal incidence) and 45 degrees are most common.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the expected lifetime of UV laser mirrors?
Lifetime depends on laser parameters (fluence, repetition rate, wavelength) and environment. With IBS coatings on JGS1 substrates at moderate fluence (< 5 J/cm2), typical lifetime exceeds 10 million pulses. Higher fluence or shorter wavelengths reduce lifetime.
Can these mirrors handle both CW and pulsed UV lasers?
Yes, but the coating design is optimized for one regime. CW mirrors prioritize thermal management; pulsed mirrors prioritize LIDT. Specify your laser mode (CW/pulsed) and parameters when ordering.
