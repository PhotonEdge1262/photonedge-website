CaF2 Plano-Convex Lenses

Calcium fluoride (CaF2) plano-convex lenses offering one of the broadest transmission ranges of any optical material, from deep UV (170nm) to mid-IR (7.8um). Exceptionally low dispersion and high laser damage threshold for demanding applications.

Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • CaF2 (UV Grade) — Broadest UV transmission of any common optical material. Ideal for excimer lasers (193nm, 248nm) and deep UV spectroscopy.
  • CaF2 (IR Grade) — Excellent transmission to 7.8um. Low refractive index reduces reflection losses. For IR applications where ZnSe is too absorptive.
  • UV Fused Silica (Alternative) — More economical for UV applications above 185nm. Higher CTE than CaF2 but similar UV durability.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • AR @ 193nm (ArF) — R < 0.25% per surface. Ion-beam sputtered for excimer laser durability.
  • AR @ 248nm (KrF) — R < 0.2% per surface. For KrF excimer laser systems.
  • BBAR UV-VIS — R < 0.5% from 200-800nm. For broadband UV-visible applications.
  • BBAR VIS-IR — R < 0.5% from 400-5000nm. For multi-band spectroscopy.

Engineering Considerations

Thermal sensitivity: CaF2 has a higher CTE (18.85 x 10^-6/K) than fused silica. For applications with significant temperature variation, account for thermal expansion in your mechanical design. Avoid rapid temperature changes which can cause thermal shock.

Cleavage planes: CaF2 has distinct cleavage planes along the {111} direction. Handle with care during mounting and avoid point contact or uneven clamping forces. Use compliant mounting methods.

Low refractive index advantage: CaF2's low refractive index (1.43) means lower Fresnel reflection losses (about 3.2% per surface uncoated vs 4% for BK7), making AR coatings more effective.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why choose CaF2 over fused silica for UV optics?
CaF2 transmits to shorter wavelengths (170nm vs 185nm) and has lower dispersion, making it preferred for excimer laser optics and applications requiring minimal chromatic aberration. CaF2 is also more durable under excimer laser irradiation.
Can CaF2 lenses be used in both UV and IR applications?
Yes. CaF2 has one of the broadest transmission ranges of any optical material (170nm-7.8um). However, for dedicated UV or IR systems, material-specific AR coatings should be optimized for your operating wavelength range.
