What Are Germanium Optical Windows?

Germanium optical windows are flat, parallel substrates cut from single-crystal germanium. They provide excellent transmission in the mid-wave IR (3–5 μm) and long-wave IR (8–14 μm) atmospheric windows, making them the standard material for thermal imaging and infrared optical systems.

With a refractive index of approximately 4.0, Germanium windows require AR coatings to achieve usable transmission. Uncoated Ge surfaces reflect approximately 36% per surface, yielding only ~41% total transmission through both faces.

Engineering Notes

Thermal Absorption

Above 100°C, Ge absorption increases significantly (dn/dT = 4.0×10⁻⁸/K). For high-power CO₂ lasers, verify thermal stability with beam diameter and duty cycle analysis.

AR Coating Required

High refractive index (~4.0) means ~36% Fresnel reflection per surface. AR coatings are mandatory for any transmission application. Uncoated transmission is only ~41%.

Opaque in Visible

Ge is completely opaque below ~2 μm. It cannot be used for visible alignment or laser sighting. Use a co-aligned visible laser for system alignment.

Weight Consideration

At 5.32 g/cm³, Ge is significantly heavier than ZnSe (5.27) or Si (2.33). Factor weight into portable or airborne thermal systems.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Ge so reflective?

Germanium has a very high refractive index (~4.0), causing ~36% Fresnel reflection per surface. This is why AR coatings are essential — without coating, only ~41% of light transmits through both surfaces.

Is Ge temperature sensitive?

Yes. The dn/dT of 4.0×10⁻⁸/K causes focus shift with temperature changes. Above 100°C, absorption increases significantly. Compensate in system design or limit operating temperature.

Can Ge windows be used in vacuum?

Yes. Germanium is compatible with vacuum environments and has relatively low outgassing. It is commonly used in space-borne IR instruments.

What sizes are available?

Standard diameters range from 5mm to 100mm with thicknesses from 1mm to 50mm. Larger sizes and custom shapes are available upon request.

How does Ge compare to ZnSe?

Ge is harder (6 vs 2.5 Mohs) and less expensive, but ZnSe offers broader transmission (0.5–20 μm) and better thermal stability for high-power applications.

Can I specify a custom shape?

Yes. Circular, rectangular, square, and custom shapes are available. Provide your drawing or dimensional requirements for a quotation.
