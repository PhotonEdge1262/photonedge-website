Germanium Optical Windows

Germanium optical windows for mid-wave and long-wave infrared systems. Excellent transmission in the 2–14µm range with high refractive index (n≈4.0), making them the standard choice for thermal imaging, FLIR systems and IR sensing.

Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • Float-zone Ge — High resistivity (>40 Ω·cm). Standard for thermal imaging and IR optics.
  • Czochralski Ge — Lower cost. Suitable for applications where resistivity is not critical.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • AR @ 8–12µm (LWIR) — R < 0.3% per surface across the atmospheric window. Essential for thermal imaging — uncoated Ge loses ~36% per surface.
  • AR @ 3–5µm (MWIR) — R < 0.3% per surface. For mid-wave IR systems.
  • Dual-band AR (3–5µm + 8–12µm) — R < 0.5% across both atmospheric windows. For multispectral IR systems.
  • ER (Enhanced Reflection) — For use as IR mirrors instead of windows.

Engineering Considerations

Temperature dependence: Germanium becomes opaque above ~150°C due to intrinsic carrier absorption. For high-temperature environments (engine exhaust, industrial furnaces), consider alternative materials or active cooling.

AR coating is mandatory: With n≈4.0, uncoated Ge reflects ~36% per surface. AR coating brings total transmission above 95%. Never deploy uncoated Ge windows in imaging systems.

Weight considerations: Ge is dense (5.32 g/cm³). For large-diameter windows in airborne systems, consider the weight impact. Silicon windows (n≈3.4, density 2.33) may be lighter alternatives for MWIR.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can germanium windows be used at high temperatures?
Ge becomes opaque above approximately 150°C due to thermal carrier generation. For environments above this temperature, consider silicon windows (usable to ~200°C) or sapphire windows for visible-NIR protection. If Ge is required at elevated temperature, active cooling of the window mount is necessary.
Why is AR coating so important for Ge windows?
Germanium has a very high refractive index (n≈4.0), causing ~36% reflection per uncoated surface. Without AR coating, a window transmits only about 29% of incident IR radiation. With dual-surface AR coating, transmission exceeds 95%.
What is the difference between Ge and Si for thermal imaging?
Both transmit in LWIR, but Ge has higher refractive index (4.0 vs 3.4) and is opaque above 150°C. Silicon is lighter, handles higher temperatures (~200°C), and transmits into MWIR. Ge provides slightly better optical performance in the 8-12µm band; Si is preferred when weight or temperature is a concern.
