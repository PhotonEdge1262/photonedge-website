CaF2 Ultrafast Laser Optics

Calcium fluoride optical components engineered for ultrafast femtosecond laser systems. Ultra-low group velocity dispersion minimizes pulse broadening, making CaF2 the material of choice for Ti:Sapphire lasers, optical parametric amplifiers, and pulse compression setups.

Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • CaF2 (Optical Grade) — Ultra-low GVD, broad transmission. Standard for ultrafast optics below 2um wavelength.
  • UV Fused Silica (Alternative) — Lower cost for pulses >50fs where some GVD is tolerable. Good for 800nm operation above 100fs.
  • BK7 (Budget) — Acceptable for pulses >200fs in non-critical beam paths. Lowest cost option.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • BBAR 700-1000nm — R < 0.2% across Ti:Sapphire tuning range. Optimized for minimal group delay dispersion.
  • BBAR 400-700nm — For OPA signal and idler paths. Ultra-broadband AR for visible.
  • BBAR 1200-1700nm — For Yb-fiber and Cr:Forsterite ultrafast systems.
  • Chirped mirror coating — Custom dispersion-compensated coatings for pulse compression.

Engineering Considerations

Dispersion management: For sub-30fs pulses, every optical element in the beam path adds group delay dispersion. Minimize the total CaF2 thickness. Use thin windows and consider reflective optics where possible to reduce accumulated dispersion.

BBAR coating design: For ultrafast applications, AR coatings must be designed for minimal group delay dispersion (GDD), not just low reflectance. Standard AR coatings can add significant chirp to <20fs pulses. Specify GDD requirements with your coating order.

CaF2 prism pairs: CaF2 is the standard prism material for dispersion compensation in Ti:Sapphire oscillers and amplifiers. Matched pairs with apex angle tolerance <1 arc-min ensure optimal compression.

Frequently Asked Questions

What pulse durations can CaF2 optics support?
CaF2 optics with appropriate BBAR coatings can support pulses down to <10fs. The key factor is accumulated GDD - minimize CaF2 thickness in the beam path and use GDD-optimized coatings for the shortest pulses.
Why is CaF2 preferred over fused silica for ultrafast lasers?
CaF2 has significantly lower group velocity dispersion (GVD) than fused silica at 800nm. For a 5mm thick window at 800nm, CaF2 adds approximately 30% less GVD than fused silica, reducing pulse broadening.
