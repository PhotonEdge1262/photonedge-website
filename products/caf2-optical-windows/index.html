CaF₂ Optical Windows

Calcium fluoride (CaF₂) optical windows offering the broadest transmission of any common optical material — from deep vacuum UV (130nm) through visible and into the mid-infrared (10µm). Exceptional choice for fluorescence, Raman spectroscopy and multi-spectral systems.

Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • Optical grade CaF₂ — Natural or synthetic CaF₂ with high purity. Standard for UV-IR broadband applications.
  • UV grade CaF₂ — Lower inclusion density for excimer laser and deep UV applications.
  • Alternative: MgF₂ — Even deeper UV transmission (115nm+), but birefringent. For polarized UV systems.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • AR @ 193nm (ArF) — R < 0.25% @ 193nm. For excimer laser and lithography.
  • AR 200–1100nm (broadband) — R < 0.5% across UV-VIS-NIR. Ideal for multi-spectral instruments.
  • AR @ specific laser lines — Optimized for 355nm, 532nm, or 1064nm as needed.
  • Uncoated — ~3.2% Fresnel loss per surface (lower than most materials due to n≈1.43).

Engineering Considerations

Thermal shock sensitivity: CaF₂ has moderate thermal shock resistance. Avoid rapid temperature changes, especially for large windows. The cleavage planes make it sensitive to mechanical shock.

Low refractive index advantage: With n≈1.43, CaF₂ has lower Fresnel losses than most optical materials (~3.2% per surface uncoated). This also means AR coatings are more durable with lower stress.

Birefringence: CaF₂ is isotropic (cubic crystal), so it has intrinsically low birefringence. This makes it ideal for applications where polarization purity matters, unlike MgF₂ which is birefringent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why choose CaF2 over fused silica for UV applications?
CaF2 transmits down to 130nm (vacuum UV), while fused silica cuts off around 160–185nm. CaF2 also has lower birefringence and lower refractive index. However, fused silica is harder, more thermally stable, and less expensive for applications above 185nm.
Is CaF2 suitable for high-power laser applications?
CaF2 has good laser damage threshold but lower thermal conductivity than fused silica. For high-average-power UV systems, fused silica is usually preferred. CaF2 excels in low-to-moderate power broadband UV-IR applications.
How should I handle and clean CaF2 windows?
CaF2 is softer than glass (Mohs 4) and has cleavage planes. Handle with gloves, avoid mechanical stress. Clean with high-purity isopropyl alcohol using gentle blotting. Never use ultrasonic cleaning on CaF2.
