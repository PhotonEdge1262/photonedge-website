Silicon Optical Windows

Single-crystal silicon windows for mid-wave infrared (MWIR) systems operating from 1.2um to 7um. Lightweight alternative to germanium with excellent thermal conductivity. Ideal for 3-5um thermal imaging and laser systems.

Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • Silicon (Si) — Lightweight MWIR material. Best for 3-5um band. Excellent thermal conductivity for high-power applications.
  • Germanium (Ge) — Higher refractive index for more compact designs. Covers both MWIR (3-5um) and LWIR (8-14um). Heavier than Si.
  • ZnSe (Alternative) — Broadband IR from 0.6um to 18um. Better when you need visible alignment beam transmission. Softer and hygroscopic at high temperature.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • AR @ 3-5um (MWIR) — R < 0.5% per surface. Standard for MWIR thermal imaging and gas sensing.
  • AR @ 1-3um (SWIR) — R < 0.5% per surface. For short-wave IR laser and imaging systems.
  • BB AR MWIR — R < 1% across 3-5um. For broadband MWIR applications.
  • DLC Overcoat — Hard protective layer for harsh environment deployments. Extends coating lifetime.

Engineering Considerations

Si vs Ge for MWIR: Silicon is preferred over germanium for MWIR (3-5um) systems when weight is a concern. Si has less than half the density of Ge (2.33 vs 5.32 g/cm3) and superior thermal conductivity (163 vs 100 W/m-K). However, Ge covers both MWIR and LWIR bands while Si is opaque beyond 7um.

AR coating is essential: With n=3.42, uncoated silicon reflects ~30% per surface. AR coating improves throughput from ~41% to >99% per element. Always specify AR coating for your operating wavelength.

Thermal advantage: Silicon's high thermal conductivity makes it ideal for high-power laser windows and applications with significant thermal loading. The material dissipates heat efficiently, reducing thermal lensing effects.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I choose silicon over germanium?
Choose silicon when your system operates in the MWIR band (3-5um) and weight or thermal management is important. Choose germanium when you need LWIR coverage (8-14um) or a higher refractive index for more compact optical designs.
Is silicon transparent at visible wavelengths?
No. Silicon is opaque below approximately 1.2um. It cannot be used for visible or near-IR applications. For alignment purposes, consider ZnSe which transmits visible red light at low efficiency.
