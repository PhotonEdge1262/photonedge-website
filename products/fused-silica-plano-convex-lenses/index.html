Fused Silica Plano-Convex Lenses

UV-grade fused silica plano-convex lenses optimized for laser beam focusing and collimation across the UV, visible and near-IR spectrum. Near-zero thermal expansion and high laser damage threshold make them ideal for demanding optical systems.

Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • UV Grade Fused Silica — High transmission 185-2500nm, near-zero CTE, high LIDT. Standard for UV and high-power laser applications.
  • N-BK7 — Economical alternative for visible-NIR focusing where UV transmission is not needed. Good optical homogeneity.
  • CaF2 — Deep UV transmission below 200nm. Low refractive index reduces reflection losses. For excimer laser systems.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • BBAR UV-VIS-NIR — R < 0.5% across 350-2000nm. Single coating for wide spectral coverage.
  • V-band AR @ 355nm — R < 0.1% @ 355nm. Optimized for Nd:YAG third harmonic systems.
  • AR @ 1064nm — R < 0.1% per surface. For Nd:YAG fundamental laser focusing.
  • Uncoated — ~4% Fresnel loss per surface. Suitable for low-power or alignment applications.

Manufacturing Tolerances

We optimize specifications to match your system requirements — not over-specify. Tighter tolerances increase cost without always improving system performance. Typical manufacturing capabilities:

Applications

Engineering Considerations

Orientation matters: For focusing a collimated beam, orient the plano-convex lens with the curved surface facing the longer conjugate distance (collimated side). This minimizes spherical aberration.

Thermal stability: Fused silica's near-zero CTE (0.55 x 10^-6/K) means focal length stays stable across temperature changes. For systems with >10 degrees C variation, fused silica outperforms BK7 significantly.

Spherical aberration: For f-numbers below f/4, consider aspheric lenses to reduce spherical aberration. Plano-convex singlets perform well above f/4.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I choose fused silica over BK7 for plano-convex lenses?
Choose fused silica when your application requires UV transmission below 350nm, operation in high-power laser systems (high LIDT needed), or thermal stability across wide temperature ranges. BK7 is more cost-effective for visible-NIR applications with moderate power levels.
What is the minimum focal length available?
Standard fused silica plano-convex lenses are available from 5mm focal length. For shorter focal lengths, consider ball lenses or aspheric lenses which achieve tighter focusing in a more compact form factor.
How do I specify the lens orientation in my system?
For best performance with a collimated input beam, orient the curved surface toward the collimated beam (longer conjugate). This minimizes spherical aberration. Mark the curved surface orientation in your assembly drawing.
