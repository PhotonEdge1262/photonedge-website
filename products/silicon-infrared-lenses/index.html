Material Options

Select the optimal optical material based on your wavelength, power, thermal environment and mechanical requirements.

  • Silicon (Si) — Lightweight MWIR material. Best thermal conductivity among IR materials. Standard for 3-5um systems.
  • Germanium (Ge) — Higher index (n=4.0) for more compact designs. Covers MWIR + LWIR. Heavier.
  • ZnSe (Alternative) — Broadband 0.6-18um. Visible alignment beam compatible. Softer material.

Coating Options

Coatings are engineered around your specific wavelength and operating conditions.

  • AR @ 3-5um (MWIR) — R < 0.5% per surface. Standard for thermal imaging systems.
  • BB AR MWIR — R < 1% across 3-5um. For broadband MWIR systems.
  • DLC Overcoat — Hard protective layer. Recommended for field-deployed systems.

Engineering Considerations

Si vs Ge for lens design: Silicon's lower refractive index (3.42 vs 4.0 for Ge) means slightly longer focal lengths for the same curvature, but the weight savings (56% lighter) is significant for large-aperture optics (>50mm diameter) and aerospace applications.

Thermal management advantage: Silicon's thermal conductivity (163 W/m-K) is 63% higher than germanium. For high-power laser or high-ambient-temperature applications, silicon lenses dissipate heat more effectively, reducing thermal lensing.

Opaque in visible: Silicon is opaque below 1.2um. Visible alignment is not possible. Use IR-sensitive cameras or coordinate measurement for system alignment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can silicon lenses replace germanium in MWIR systems?
Yes, for systems operating exclusively in the 3-5um band. Silicon offers weight savings and better thermal management. Choose germanium only if you also need LWIR (8-14um) coverage or need the highest refractive index for compact designs.
What focal lengths are available?
We manufacture silicon PCX, meniscus and aspheric lenses from 10mm to 500mm focal length. Custom designs are available for specific system requirements.
