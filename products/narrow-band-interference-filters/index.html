What Is a Narrow Band Interference Filters?

Narrow band interference filters selectively transmit a specific wavelength band while blocking all other wavelengths. They work on the principle of thin-film interference, using precisely controlled multi-layer dielectric coatings to create constructive interference at the target wavelength and destructive interference elsewhere.

These filters can replace expensive spectrometers in applications where only a specific wavelength band needs to be isolated. They are widely used in fluorescence microscopy, Raman spectroscopy, laser line selection, environmental monitoring, and machine vision inspection systems.

When Should You Use This Product?

✔ Good Choice When

  • ✓ Isolating specific fluorescence emission bands in microscopy
  • ✓ Raman spectroscopy signal extraction (rejecting Rayleigh scatter)
  • ✓ Laser line selection and cleanup
  • ✓ Machine vision inspection at specific illumination wavelengths

⚠ Consider an Alternative When

✗ Broad wavelength bandpass needed (> 100nm) → Longpass or Shortpass Filter
✗ Variable attenuation rather than wavelength selection → Neutral Density Filter
✗ Multiple wavelength bands simultaneously → Dichroic Filter / Multiband Filter
✗ Very high spectral resolution (< 0.1nm) → Grating-based spectrometer

Engineering Considerations

Key factors to evaluate when specifying this optical component for your system.

Center Wavelength (CWL)

The peak transmission wavelength shifts toward shorter wavelengths as the angle of incidence increases. For non-normal incidence, specify the AOI so we can design the coating accordingly.

Bandwidth (FWHM)

Narrower bandwidth provides better spectral isolation but reduces total transmitted energy and increases cost. Typical choices: 1-3nm for Raman, 10-20nm for fluorescence, 20-40nm for machine vision.

Blocking (OD)

Optical density defines how well out-of-band light is suppressed. OD 3 means 0.1% transmission outside the passband. For fluorescence applications, OD 4-6 is often required to suppress excitation light.

Peak Transmission

Higher peak transmission means more signal. Narrow bandwidth designs trade peak transmission for selectivity. Typical values: 40-60% for narrow (< 5nm), 70-90% for moderate (10-40nm).

Angle of Incidence

For converging or diverging beams, the effective AOI varies across the beam, causing bandwidth broadening and center wavelength shift. Specify the beam cone angle for accurate performance prediction.

Temperature Stability

Dielectric coatings shift approximately 0.01-0.02 nm/°C. For precision applications in varying temperatures, specify the operating temperature range.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between narrow band and broadband filters?

Narrow band filters have bandwidth (FWHM) typically below 40nm and isolate a specific wavelength. Broadband filters pass a wider range of wavelengths. Narrow band filters are more selective but transmit less total energy.

Can I specify a custom center wavelength?

Yes. PhotonEdge designs custom narrow band filters for any center wavelength from 200nm to 2500nm. Provide your target wavelength, bandwidth, and blocking requirements for a custom design.

How does angle of incidence affect filter performance?

As the angle of incidence increases, the center wavelength shifts toward shorter wavelengths (blue shift). For converging beams, this causes bandwidth broadening. Always specify your beam geometry for accurate performance.

What does OD 4 blocking mean?

OD (Optical Density) 4 means the filter blocks 99.99% of light outside the passband (transmission < 0.01%). Higher OD values provide better blocking but may reduce peak transmission and increase cost.

What substrate should I choose?

For visible wavelengths (400-700nm), BK7 is the most economical choice. For UV center wavelengths (< 400nm), UV Fused Silica is required. For economy visible-only applications, B270 borosilicate glass is available.

Can these filters handle high-power laser?

Standard dielectric filters can handle moderate laser powers. For high-power applications, specify the laser power density and we will design with high-LIDT hard dielectric coatings.

What is the typical lead time for custom filters?

Standard catalog filters ship within 1-2 weeks. Custom narrow band filter designs typically require 3-4 weeks for development and production.

How do I specify a filter for fluorescence?

Provide: (1) excitation wavelength, (2) emission peak wavelength, (3) required bandwidth, (4) blocking OD at excitation wavelength, and (5) whether it is for excitation or emission path. We will recommend the optimal design.
