Optical Challenges for Aerospace & Defense

Understanding the failure modes is the first step in specifying the right optic. Here are the dominant engineering challenges in aerospace & defense systems.

Extreme Temperature Cycling

Aerospace and defense systems operate across wide temperature ranges — from -55°C to +85°C or wider for some applications. Thermal expansion changes optical path length, causes defocus, and can introduce stress birefringence. Athermalized design and material selection are critical.

Vibration & Shock Survival

Optics in aircraft, missile, and ground vehicle systems must survive severe vibration and shock. Substrate strength, mounting method, and coating adhesion all affect survivability. Environmental qualification per MIL-STD-810 or similar standards is often required.

High Vacuum & Outgassing

Space and high-altitude optics must meet strict outgassing requirements (e.g., ASTM E595). Materials and coatings can't release volatile compounds that would contaminate sensors or other system components. Low-outgassing epoxies and hermetic packaging may be required.

IR Broadband Performance

EO/IR systems operate across multiple IR bands — MWIR (3–5 μm), LWIR (8–14 μm), and often visible/NIR for targeting. Optics must perform consistently across bands. Multi-band AR coatings and achromatic IR lens designs add complexity.

Radiation Resistance

Space and high-altitude optics are exposed to ionizing radiation that can darken optical materials (radiation darkening) and change refractive index. Radiation-resistant materials and radiation testing qualification are essential for space programs.

Athermalized Design

Maintaining focus and image quality across the full operating temperature range requires athermal optical design. Choosing materials with complementary thermal properties (thermal expansion, dn/dT) and mechanical design that compensates for thermal effects is essential for defense-grade systems.

Material Selection Guide

Recommended substrate materials for aerospace & defense applications, with their key advantages and typical use cases.

Material Why It's Used in Aerospace & Defense Key Benefit
Zinc Selenide (ZnSe)
0.5 μm – 20 μm
 Standard IR material for MWIR and LWIR optics. Good transmission from visible through LWIR enables visible sighting paths with IR imaging. Used in FLIR, targeting pods, and IR countermeasures. Broad IR + visible transmission
Germanium (Ge)
2 μm – 16 μm
 High refractive index (~4.0) enables compact high-performance IR lens designs. The standard for LWIR imaging and targeting systems. Available with diamond-turned surfaces for aspheric IR optics. High index for compact LWIR designs
Silicon (Si)
1.2 μm – 7 μm
 Excellent thermal conductivity — among the best of optical materials. Used for near-IR and MWIR optics where thermal management matters. Lower cost than ZnSe or Ge. Common in LIDAR and SWIR systems. Best thermal conductivity IR material
Sapphire
150 nm – 5.5 μm
 Extreme hardness and strength make it ideal for aerodynamic windows and domes. Transmits from UV through mid-IR. Used in missile seekers, aircraft windows, and harsh-environment sensors. Maximum strength & durability
Fused Silica
185 nm – 2.5 μm
 Excellent thermal stability and good radiation resistance make it the standard for visible-NIR space optics and airborne laser systems. Ultra-low CTE minimizes thermal defocus. Low CTE + radiation resistance

Section 4

Coating Recommendations

Coatings tailored to aerospace & defense operating conditions — from high-LIDT dielectric stacks to broadband metallic reflectors.

1

Multi-Band IR AR Coatings

Anti-reflection coatings designed for multiple IR bands (e.g., MWIR + LWIR, or visible + IR). Diamond-like carbon (DLC) coatings provide durable IR AR with scratch resistance for exposed optics.

Typical uses: EO/IR systems, targeting pods, FLIR

2

Ruggedized Hard Coatings

Durable coatings formulated for environmental survivability — rain erosion, salt spray, sand/dust. DLC (diamond-like carbon) and other hard coatings protect exposed IR windows and domes.

Typical uses: Exposed windows, domes, aircraft optics

3

High-Power Laser Coatings

High-LIDT coatings for airborne laser systems and directed energy applications. Designed for maximum damage threshold at design wavelength with proper thermal management.

Typical uses: Airborne lasers, directed energy, LIDAR

4

Low-Outgassing Space Coatings

Coatings formulated and cured to meet ASTM E595 outgassing requirements for space applications. Minimal volatile content prevents contamination of space-based sensors.

Typical uses: Satellite optics, space sensors

Frequently Asked Questions

Germanium is the standard for LWIR (8–14 μm) imaging. Its high refractive index (~4.0) enables compact lens designs with fewer elements. Zinc sulfide and multispectral ZnS are alternatives for systems requiring both visible and LWIR transmission.

Critical. Defense systems typically operate across -55°C to +85°C or wider. Temperature changes cause defocus through both thermal expansion and thermo-optic effects (dn/dT). Athermalized optical design — using materials with complementary thermal properties — is essential for maintaining performance across the full temperature range.

NASA SP-8007 and ASTM E595 define standard outgassing tests: Total Mass Loss (TML) < 1.0% and Collected Volatile Condensable Materials (CVCM) < 0.1%. These limits prevent contamination of sensitive spacecraft surfaces. Both materials and coatings must meet these specifications.

Sapphire has excellent mechanical strength and is used for aircraft windows and missile domes. However, design details — thickness, edge support, pressure differential, and thermal gradient — all determine actual survivability. PhotonEdge works with structural engineers to specify appropriate sapphire window designs for demanding applications.

For exposed IR windows that face environmental hazards (rain, dust, salt spray), diamond-like carbon (DLC) coatings provide the best durability. DLC is a hard, scratch-resistant coating that also provides anti-reflection performance in the IR. It's the standard choice for aircraft and ground vehicle IR windows.
