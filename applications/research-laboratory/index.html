Optical Challenges for Research & Laboratory

Understanding the failure modes is the first step in specifying the right optic. Here are the dominant engineering challenges in research & laboratory systems.

Broad Wavelength Coverage

Research labs work across the full optical spectrum — from deep UV to far IR. A single lab may need optics for 193 nm excimer lasers one week and 10.6 μm CO2 experiments the next. Having access to the full range of optical materials and coatings without minimum order quantities is essential.

Custom Specifications

Research often requires non-standard dimensions, unusual coatings, or tight tolerances that don't fit catalog offerings. Custom fabrication with fast turnaround and low minimum order quantity enables experimental flexibility. One-off prototypes should be available without prohibitive setup costs.

Fast Prototyping Turnaround

Research timelines are tight. Waiting 4-6 weeks for custom optics kills experimental momentum. Fast prototype turnaround — typically 2-4 weeks for standard custom components — enables researchers to iterate quickly and maintain project momentum.

Low Volume / Single Piece

Many research experiments need only one or two pieces of a particular optic. Traditional optical manufacturers impose minimum order quantities (MOQs) that make small-quantity research orders expensive or impossible. No-MOQ custom optics are critical for research.

Consistent Quality Across Orders

Experimental reproducibility requires consistent optical performance from one order to the next. Documented processes, measured test reports, and traceable materials ensure that the replacement optic matches the original — essential for long-running experiments.

Technical Support & Consulting

Researchers are experts in their field but may not be optical engineers. Getting knowledgeable technical support during specification prevents costly mistakes. Material selection advice, coating optimization, and tolerance recommendations should come standard with every order.

Material Why It's Used in Research & Laboratory Key Benefit
BK7 / N-BK7
350 nm – 2 μm
 The laboratory workhorse. Excellent general-purpose glass for visible and near-IR experiments. Readily available in all standard forms and can be custom fabricated quickly and economically. Most versatile & cost-effective
UV Fused Silica
185 nm – 2.5 μm
 Standard for UV and high-power laser experiments. Excellent homogeneity and low thermal expansion. Available in both standard and UV-grade qualities depending on wavelength requirements. UV + laser + thermal stability

130 nm – 8 μm
 Deep UV and low-dispersion applications. Essential for excimer laser research, deep UV spectroscopy, and applications requiring minimal chromatic aberration. Deep UV + low dispersion
ZnSe
0.5 μm – 20 μm
 Mid-IR and CO2 laser experiments. Broad IR transmission with visible sighting capability. Standard substrate for 10.6 μm CO2 laser research. Broad IR + visible sighting
Sapphire
150 nm – 5.5 μm
 When durability, chemical resistance, or extreme environment matters. Used in harsh chemistry experiments, high-pressure cells, and UV-IR broadband setups. Hardest & most durable

Coating Recommendations

Coatings tailored to research & laboratory operating conditions — from high-LIDT dielectric stacks to broadband metallic reflectors.

1

Standard AR Coatings

Single-wavelength and broadband AR coatings for common research wavelengths (248 nm, 355 nm, 532 nm, 633 nm, 785 nm, 1064 nm, etc.). Available on all standard substrate materials with fast turnaround.

Typical uses: General lab optics, laser experiments

2

Broadband AR Coatings

Multi-band AR coatings for spectroscopy and broadband experiments. Common bands: 350–700 nm (UV-Vis), 400–1000 nm (Vis-NIR), 2–5 μm (MWIR).

Typical uses: Spectroscopy, broadband imaging

3

Laser Line HR Coatings

High-reflectance dielectric coatings for common laser lines. >99% reflectivity at design wavelength. Available for 0° and 45° incidence angles.

Typical uses: Laser cavities, beam steering

4

Custom Coating Design

Need a coating for an unusual wavelength or angle? We design and deposit custom coatings for research applications — from narrow UV lines to broadband IR ranges. Small-batch runs available.

Typical uses: Specialized research, custom experiments
